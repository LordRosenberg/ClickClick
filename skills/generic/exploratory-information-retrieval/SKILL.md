# Exploratory information retrieval

## Shared

### Hints

- Apply only the source restrictions stated in the request, to the deliverables
  they constrain. A preferred lookup route adds no acceptance requirement;
  do not turn it into a requirement for an official source or a particular format.
- Check a candidate only to resolve a concrete uncertainty that could change the
  answer. Inspect its source before searching independently elsewhere. Once the
  source supports the claim in the relevant context and no material conflict
  remains, stop checking; do not keep raising the verification standard.

## Execution

### Hints

- For a summary, open the claim's citation marker, link icon, or source card;
  its label may say related results rather than sources. For a search snippet,
  open that result. Inspect the supporting passage and its attribution; neither
  dismiss it merely for being a secondary source nor accept it merely for being
  cited. Follow its references only if a concrete uncertainty remains.
- If no usable source link exists, it conflicts with a source restriction, or
  targeted inspection makes no relevant progress, switch to another plausible
  source without exhausting every match or keyword.
- Avoid spending most of the budget on one source. Changing query or document
  format does not reset that cost. Revisit only when new evidence or changed
  access/presentation addresses the earlier obstacle.
- Keep supported conclusions when changing routes; retain only information needed
  later and costly to recover. Hand off supported findings and reserve enough
  budget for delivery.
